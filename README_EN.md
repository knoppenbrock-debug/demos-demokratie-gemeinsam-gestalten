# DEMOS – Shaping Democracy Together

**Discussion model 2026 · Red-Team version 0.1 · 4 October 2026**

> **Language note:** The authoritative project text is German. This English summary is provided for international discussion, searchability and machine readability.  
> **[Read the authoritative German version](README.md)**

## Purpose

DEMOS explores how Germany could combine **representative, direct and deliberative democracy** in one constitutional architecture.

Its core question is:

> **How can citizens receive binding agenda-setting, initiative, correction and decision rights without weakening the rule of law, human dignity, minority protection, federalism, parliamentary accountability or institutional capacity to act?**

DEMOS is **not** a proposal to abolish parliaments or political parties. It is a constitutional-design hypothesis for supplementing representative institutions with carefully defined direct-democratic rights and deliberative review.

## Current legal baseline

Article 20(2) of the German Basic Law refers to elections and votes as forms through which state authority is exercised by the people. Germany's federal system nevertheless remains predominantly representative; general federal popular legislation is not currently established.

Under Article 79(2), constitutional amendments require two-thirds of the members of the Bundestag and two-thirds of the votes of the Bundesrat. Article 79(3) protects, among other things, the principles in Articles 1 and 20, the federal structure and the fundamental participation of the Länder in legislation.

DEMOS therefore describes a **proposed constitutional architecture**, not current law.

## Core design

| Instrument | DEMOS working design | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Agenda initiative | 100,000 verified supporters | settled design element |
| Optional referendum against federal legislation | 0.5% of the electorate at the previous Bundestag election | settled design element |
| Popular initiative for ordinary federal legislation | 1% of the electorate | settled design element |
| Popular initiative for a constitutional amendment | 2% of the electorate | settled design element |
| Turnout quorum | none | settled design element |
| Support collection | verified analogue and digital support | settled; does **not** imply online voting |
| Parliamentary counterproposal | initiative and counterproposal receive separate Yes/No votes; a tie-break question decides if both pass | settled design element |
| Citizens' review | mandatory for constitutional amendments; intended for complex nationwide proposals | settled design element |
| Parliament-initiated constitutional amendment | 2/3 Bundestag + 2/3 Bundesrat + mandatory popular approval | settled target model |
| Citizen-initiated constitutional amendment | nationwide popular majority + additional federal/Länder support | principle settled; exact federal threshold open |
| 28/69 federal votes | investigated in an internal stress simulation | **open high-risk design value, not claimed to be constitutionally settled** |

## Counterproposal mechanism

A successful popular legislative initiative enters legal and constitutional review and public parliamentary debate. Parliament may adopt it, reject it or present a direct counterproposal.

Where a popular vote remains necessary:
- the initiative is voted on Yes/No;
- the counterproposal is voted on Yes/No;
- voters may approve both;
- if both are approved, a separate tie-break question determines which version prevails;
- if both are rejected, the status quo remains.

This mechanism is inspired by Swiss practice but is not intended as a copy of the Swiss constitutional system.

## Constitutional amendments

For parliament-initiated amendments, DEMOS retains the current high parliamentary and federal thresholds and adds mandatory popular approval.

For citizen-initiated amendments, DEMOS currently requires:
- 2% verified support;
- constitutional admissibility review;
- mandatory independent deliberative review;
- nationwide popular majority;
- an additional federal/Länder support requirement.

The exact Länder threshold remains unresolved. A value of **28 out of 69 Article-51-weighted federal votes** has been explored as a possible balance between federal protection and excessive veto power, but is **not** treated as a final or constitutionally validated rule.

## Protected constitutional core

Direct democracy is not modelled as unlimited majority power. The protected core includes in particular:
- human dignity;
- the structural principles of Articles 1 and 20;
- democracy and the rule of law;
- federal statehood;
- the continued existence of the Länder as constituent states;
- their fundamental participation in legislation.

A total constitutional replacement under Article 146 is treated as a separate constituent process rather than a routine workaround.

## Deliberative citizens' review

Every constitutional amendment should receive at least one independent review. The current working value is a **120-person stratified random citizens' panel**.

Its function is to hear competing expertise and publish a structured account of facts, disagreements, expected consequences and uncertainties. It has **no veto and no law-making power**.

## Open questions

Key unresolved areas include:
- the exact federal safeguard for citizen-initiated constitutional amendments;
- procedural and referendum deadlines;
- collisions between competing initiatives;
- standstill periods after popular decisions;
- campaign-finance and political-advertising rules;
- governance of an independent DEMOS administrative office;
- calibration of citizens' review;
- verified digital support infrastructure;
- interaction with EU law and subsidiarity.
